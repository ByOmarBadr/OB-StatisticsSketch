THE BIG IDEA

What exactly is statistical inference?

You begin with a research question. A properly selected sample provides estimates; probability describes how they vary from sample to sample. From there, you can estimate a population value or test a claim about it.

Example: student exam scores. We sample 20 students, calculate a mean of 70, and use a 95% t interval (approximately 65.3–74.7 if s = 10) to express uncertainty about the population mean. We do not conclude that 95% of individual scores lie in that interval.

THE FIRST INFERENTIAL SKILL · RANGE ESTIMATION

From a point estimate to a confidence interval

A single sample average is one number. A confidence interval adds a margin of error around it. With a 95% procedure, about 95% of intervals from repeated random samples would capture the fixed population mean (under the method's assumptions).

Useful idea: Larger independent samples usually narrow intervals, but a biased sample can stay wrong however large it becomes.
